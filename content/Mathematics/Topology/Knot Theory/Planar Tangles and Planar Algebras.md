As we have seen in [[Tangles and Categories of Tangles|tangles]] and [[The Braid Category|braids]], though they are purely topological objects, they carry strong information to describe very abstract algebraic notions. We will see that the same is true for planar tangles and planar algebras, for which can be defined algebraically through *operads*.

## Planar Tangles

> [!definition] Planar Tangle
> A *planar tangle*  is a tuple $(T,X,Q)$ consisting of
> -  $T = D^{2}\setminus \left( \bigsqcup_{i=1}^{m} D_{i} \right)$, where $D^{2}\subset \C$ is the closed unit disk, and $\{D_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}$ are disjoint closed disks contained in the interior of $D^{2}$, called *internal disks* of $T$;
> - $X$ is a closed 1-dimensional submanifold of $T$ with boundary $\partial X\subset \partial T$, called the *strings* of $T$;
> - A set $Q = \{q_{0}, \cdots, q_{r}\}$, q0 = q, qi ∈ ∂iT \ X, called the anchor points.
>
