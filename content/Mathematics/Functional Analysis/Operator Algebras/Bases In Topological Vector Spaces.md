In a topological vector space, aside from algebraic structures, we also have topological structures. In particular, we can define a *Schauder basis* for a topological vector space, which is a generalization of the concept of a [[Vector Spaces#^4baae2|(Hamel) basis]] in linear algebra.

> [!definition] Total Basis
> Suppose $X$ is a topological vector space. A subset $B\subset X$ is a total basis if its [[Vector Spaces#^concept-4e452455341d|span]] is [[Mathematics/Topology/General Topology/Closure, Interior and Boundary#^b560bf|dense]] in $X$ but no proper subset of $B$ has a dense span. ^concept-993c30d7b93f

> [!definition] Schauder Basis
> Suppose $X$ is a topological vector space. A sequence $\{x_n\}_{n=1}^{\infty}$ in $X$ is called a *Schauder basis* if for every $x \in X$, there exists a unique sequence of scalars $\{a_n\}_{n=1}^{\infty}$ such that $x=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} a_n x_n$, where the series (i.e., [[Countability Axioms and Nets#^ed5107|net]]) converges in the topology of $X$.
>
