The study of factors is as natural a subdiscipline of von Neumann algebra theory as the study of simple groups or rings is in abstract algebra. Indeed, we will see that a von Neumann algebra is a factor if and only if it contains no strongly-closed ideals.

> [!definition] Factor
> A *factor* is a von Neumann algebra $M$ with trivial center, i.e. $Z(M)=\mathbb{C}1$ where $1$ is the identity. ^c1e2d6

Note that factoriality is equivalent to a simplicity requirement! Thus, the study of factors is as natural a subdiscipline of von Neumann algebra theory as the study of simple groups or rings is in abstract algebra

> [!proposition]
> A von Neumann algebra is a factor if and only if it contains no strongly-closed ideals.
> 

## Classification of Factors

> [!lemma]
> The Murray-von Neumann order in a factor $M$ is a [[Preorder, Partial Order and Posets#^a8688d|total order]] on Murray-von Neumann equivalence classes.

> [!theorem]
> Suppose $M$ is a factor. Then the Murray-von Neumann order on equivalence classes in $M$ can only be isomorphic to a finite total order, or $\newcommand{\N}{\mathbb{N}}\N\cup\{\infty\}$, or $[0,1]\cong [0,\infty]$, or $\{0,1\}\cong\{0,\infty\}$. According to that, the *factor type* of $M\newcommand{\I}{\text{I}}$ is defined by the following table:
> 
> | Order Type | [[Mathematics/Functional Analysis/Operator Algebras/Murray-von Neumann Ordering#^ac7c0d\|Finiteness]]                          | Factor Type                                   | 
> | --------------------- | ------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------- | 
> | $\{1,2,\cdots,n\}$                |  finite    |   $\newcommand{\I}{\text{I}}\I_{n}$                                     |
> | $\N\cup \{\infty\}$                  | infinite | $\I_{\infty}$  | 
> | $[0,1]$                  | finite | $\I\I_{1}$  | 
> | $[0, \infty]$ | infinite | $\I\I_{\infty}$  | 
> | $\{0,1\}$              | infinite | $\I\I\I$  | 
> 
> ^f5c2e9

## $\I\I_{2}$ factors

> [!proposition]
> A factor is of type $\I\I_{2}$ iff it is infinite-dimensional and tracial.
>
