## Multiply Transitive Groups

> [!definition] $n$-Transitivity
> Suppose $X$ is a set and $G$ is a group acting on $X$. The action of $G$ on $X$ is called *$n$-transitive* if the induced action on $X^{n}$ is [[Group Actions#^065d30|transitive]]. That is, for any two $n$-tuples $(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})$ and $(y_{1},\dots,y_{n})$ of distinct elements of $X$, there exists an element $g\in G$ such that $g\cdot x_{i}=y_{i}$ for all $i=1,\dots,n$. If, in addition, such $g$ is unique, then the action is called *sharply $n$-transitive*.
> A group $G$ is called *$n$-transitive* if it admits an $n$-transitive action on some set $X$. ^f455f4
